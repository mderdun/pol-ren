# Pronunciation: sources to get

Sources for checking and extending `pronunciation-guide.tex`. Each entry says what it is for and where it is likely to be found. Entries marked *confirmed* were checked to exist; the rest are standard references named in the literature but not yet seen.

## Latin as sung in Poland

The weakest part of the guide: no published account of how Polish choirs pronounced Latin c. 1500–1650 has been found yet. The guide rests on Polish loanwords and on the later church and school tradition.

1. **Wikipedia, "Latin regional pronunciation"** (en) — the Polish column, already used. Cites Wikarjak 1979 and Jurewicz et al. 2004 (below). *confirmed*
2. **Wikipedia, "Tradycyjna wymowa łacińska"** (pl) — the Polish traditional pronunciation; says classrooms prefer *ti* and church usage *tsi*; calls voiced intervocalic *s* "an error" (uncited). Useful for the modern tradition, not for 1550. *confirmed*
3. **Wikarjak, Jan.** *Gramatyka opisowa języka łacińskiego*. Warsaw: PWN, 1979, pp. 7–8. Polish school pronunciation. Cited in *Nunc scio vere*. *confirmed*
4. **Jurewicz, Oktawiusz, Lidia Winniczuk and Jadwiga Żuławska.** *Język łaciński: podręcznik dla lektoratów szkół wyższych*. 25th edn. Warsaw: PWN, 2004, pp. 13–16. As above. *confirmed*
5. **German, Jan.** *Drogi przenikania zapożyczeń greckich i łacińskich do języka staropolskiego: leksyka z dziedziny życia duchowego*. PhD dissertation, Uniwersytet Jagielloński, 2023. Open access in the Jagiellonian repository (ruj.uj.edu.pl). How Latin was read in medieval Poland (*lectio* > *lekcyja*, voiced *s*, *g* = *j*). The best single source found. *confirmed*
6. **Urbańczyk, Stanisław.** "Z zagadnień staropolskich, 4: Ańjoł, jenerał itp." *Język Polski* 32 (1952): 127–29. *G* before *e, i* read as *j*. *Język Polski* back issues are on the journal's site. *confirmed*
7. **Liban, Jerzy (Georgius Libanus Lignicensis).** *De accentuum ecclesiasticorum exquisita ratione*. Kraków: Scharffenberg, c. 1539. Church accentuation taught in Kraków; may say more about pronunciation than stress. Check Polona and the Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna entry. *confirmed (exists)*
8. **Sebastian z Felsztyna.** Treatises on chant (Kraków, 1510s), cited in *Nunc scio vere* for accent. Worth checking for remarks on reading Latin. Polona.
9. **Copeman, Harold.** *Singing in Latin, or, Pronunciation Explor'd*. Oxford: the author, 1990; rev. edn 1992. Covers national pronunciations of Latin; **whether it has a Polish section is unverified**. Library or second-hand. *confirmed (exists)*
10. **Svoboda, Čeněk.** "Výslovnost latiny v duchovní vokální hudbě renesance a baroka." *Aura musica* 9 (2016). Latin pronunciation in Renaissance and Baroque sacred music, from Ústí (Czech). Bohemian practice is the nearest neighbour to compare. aura.ujep.cz. *confirmed*
11. **McGee, Timothy J., ed., with A. G. Rigg and David N. Klausner.** *Singing Early Music*. Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 1996. No Polish chapter, but its German and Low Countries Latin chapters are a model for what a Polish one would contain. *confirmed*
12. **CD booklets** of Polish ensembles who sing "Polish Latin" (e.g. Capella Cracoviensis, Bornus Consort, Il Giardino d'Amore, Octava Ensemble, Schola Gregoriana Pragensis for Bohemia). None checked yet.
13. **Polish musicology on performance practice**: search *Muzyka*, *Muzyka Religijna w Polsce*, *Studia Źródłoznawcze*, *Polski Rocznik Muzykologiczny* for "wymowa łaciny". Nothing found online yet; a library catalogue search may do better.

## Polish of the sixteenth century and Old Polish

All the historical claims in the guide come from these, via secondary summaries. Page numbers to be checked against copies before they are cited.

14. **Długosz-Kurczabowa, Krystyna, and Stanisław Dubisz.** *Gramatyka historyczna języka polskiego*. Warsaw: WUW, 2006. Narrowed vowels (pp. 126–31), stress (p. 133). The standard textbook. *confirmed*
15. **Klemensiewicz, Zenon.** *Historia języka polskiego*. Warsaw: PWN (one-volume edns 1976, 1985, 1999). Middle Polish phonology, *ł*, *rz*, stress. *confirmed*
16. **Walczak, Bogdan.** *Zarys dziejów języka polskiego*. Wrocław: WUWr, 1999. Stress shift and the stress group with enclitics (pp. 79–81, 233). *confirmed*
17. **Kuraszkiewicz, Władysław.** *Gramatyka historyczna języka polskiego*. Warsaw: PZWS, 1972. Narrowed vowels, *rz*, nasals. *confirmed*
18. **Klemensiewicz, Zenon, Tadeusz Lehr-Spławiński and Stanisław Urbańczyk.** *Gramatyka historyczna języka polskiego*. Warsaw: PWN, 1964. The older standard; narrowed vowels pp. 85–100.
19. **Stieber, Zdzisław.** *A Historical Phonology of the Polish Language*. Heidelberg: Winter, 1973. In English; compact and reliable on nasals and *rz*.
20. **Rospond, Stanisław.** *Gramatyka historyczna języka polskiego*. Warsaw: PWN, 1971 (later reprints). Loanword adaptation (*-yja*).
21. **Wikipedia (pl): "Samogłoski pochylone", "Historia ortografii polskiej", "Rz (dwuznak)"**. Good summaries with citations; used to check the claims above. *confirmed*

## Period grammars and orthographies (primary)

These say how the language sounded in their authors' time. Most are on Polona.

22. **Zaborowski, Stanisław.** *Ortographia seu modus recte scribendi et legendi Polonicum idioma*. Kraków, c. 1514–18. The first Polish orthography.
23. **Murzynowski, Stanisław.** *Ortografija polska*. Königsberg, 1551. Proposed marking all narrowed vowels; contemporary with *Już się zmierzka*.
24. **Statorius-Stojeński, Piotr.** *Polonicae grammatices institutio*. Kraków, 1568. The first Polish grammar; clear and narrowed *a* (*bábá, spáć* / *pan, sad*). *confirmed*
25. **Januszowski, Jan.** *Nowy karakter polski*. Kraków, 1594. With Kochanowski's and Górnicki's proposals; separate letters for vowel qualities and for *rz*.
26. **Knapski, Grzegorz.** *Thesaurus polono-latino-graecus*. Kraków, 1621. First explicit testimony on Polish stress.
27. **Meniński, Franciszek (Mesgnien).** *Grammatica seu institutio Polonicae linguae*. Gdańsk, 1649. Describes the narrowed *a* for foreigners.

## Modern diction (for comparison)

28. **Kafka-Price, Laura, and Maksymilian Krzak.** *Polish Pronunciation Guide for Singers: Szymanowski, Rymy dziecięce*. Oxford University Press, 2025. Modern lyric diction for English-speaking singers; useful as a model for how to describe Polish sounds, not for history. *confirmed*
