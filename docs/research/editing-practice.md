# Editing early music for scholars and performers: best practice, and how Polish Early Music compares

Prepared 3 October 2026 for the Polish Early Music series (ed. Mikołaj Derduń).

**What I examined.** `docs/editorial-principles.md`, `docs/house-style.md`, and both PDFs of *Już się zmierzka* (critical, 9 pp.; performance, 3 pp.), rendered and read page by page. I also checked the *Vox in Rama* critical PDF for its chiavette argument. Chant (*Bogurodzica*) was not examined in detail.

**How to read the markers.** **[Consensus]** means the major sources and series agree. **[Contested]** means reputable scholars or series disagree, so a series can choose either way if it gives a reason. **[Practice]** means common house practice or practitioners' views, not a theoretical position. Where I could not get at a source, I say so in section 4. I have not paraphrased anything I could not read.

## 1. Best practice: a short synthesis

### 1.1 Editing as criticism; sources and stemma

- **Editing is critical and interpretative, and no edition is definitive.** Grier sets out four principles: editing is critical; criticism rests on historical inquiry; editing evaluates what the notation meant in its time; and "the final arbiter is the editor's conception of musical style" (Grier, Grove "Editing", §2; Grier 1996, ch. 1). Bent: "Making a good edition is essentially an act of criticism" (Bent 1986, p. 87, quoted in Lee 2013, pp. 28–9). Brett's essay is what started this line of thinking, with its demand to unmask "critically based assumptions and perceptions that usually go unacknowledged" (Brett 1988, quoted in Grier, Grove §1). **[Consensus]**
- **Know every source and how they relate.** A-R requires the critical report to "identify all known sources, describe relationships among them, and establish the basis for selecting the principal source(s)" (A-R Style Guide 2025, p. 11). Grier treats stemmatic filiation as "a powerful tool" but notes that "any stemma based on textual evidence alone is built on assumption and probability". He also says a heavily contaminated tradition calls for a single "best text" that the stemma explains (Grove §§4–5; Grier 1995). **[Consensus]**
- **Single base source, no conflation.** Caldwell: "the most objective method of presentation is likely to be that which takes a single source as its point of reference" (Caldwell 1985, p. 5, quoted in Lee 2013, p. 27). Fontes Musicae in Polonia (FMP) forbids *editio optima* except to reconstruct incomplete sources, and asks for "minimum interference" (FMP Instructions §§5–8). Taruskin objects that a single-source edition "can in effect spuriously elevate a single chance redaction to the status of authority" (Taruskin 1985–6/1995, p. 84, quoted in Lee 2013, p. 28). **[Consensus on practice, Contested in theory]**
- **Emend sparingly, and avoid "improving" the composer.** "An editor should not be open to the charge of printing the piece the composer would have written had he or she known as much as the editor" (Grove §5). FMP: correct "only in case of indisputably erroneous entries"; do not correct "imperfections related to the composer's technique or local performance practice" (§8). **[Consensus]**

### 1.2 Transcription and note values

- **Unreduced values are now the scholarly norm, but by no means universal.** The New Josquin Edition chose "unreduced note values rather than in 2:1 reduction", which set a new standard (Ory 2025, p. 505). Bent favours "original values but modern note shapes" and warns against changing the reduction ratio within a piece (Bent 2013, pp. 245–8). Lost&Found (Lisbon) keeps original values. CMM still halves: in Weerbeke, CMM 106, 2016, "a semibreve correlates with a half note". The Archive of Iberian Polyphony allows either "depending on the usual practice of each editor". Practical publishers such as Ancient Groove usually halve. Church choir directors are split (MusicaSacra forum). **[Contested; scholarly trend towards unreduced]**
- **Finals.** Lost&Found: "Final notes, regardless of their original form, take the shape of a longa". CMM 106: "breves or longae, with the implication of indeterminate duration". **[Practice]**

### 1.3 Barring

- Choices range from full modern barring (CMM 106, which leaves a barline "open" when a breve crosses it), through on-staff barlines placed by mensuration (Lost&Found, Iberian Polyphony), to Mensurstriche (allowed by FMP §19 "in justified cases ... before 1600"). JRP avoids "quixotic barring" so that editions stay comparable with the sources. **[Contested]**
- Performers' views are mixed. One experienced director writes that "choirs expect real barlines and the same number of beats in every measure". Hughes advises against Mensurstriche "for American choirs" (CMAA 2021). Scholars stress that the barline must not be read as a stress (Tagg 1965; Bent 1994, p. 387 on "unit reference"). **[Contested]**

### 1.4 Clefs and pitch

- **Modern clefs are accepted.** Bent: "modern clefs do less violence to the music than some other changes" (2013). Original clefs go in an incipit or prefatory staff: CMM 106 gives "original clefs, key signatures, and the range of each voice", and FMP §21 requires an incipit for pre-1700 works. **[Consensus]**
- **Critical editions should not transpose.** "Critical editions should normally not impose transpositions" (Bent 2013, p. 252), and Bent prefers to speak of "frequency selection". Lost&Found and Iberian Polyphony: "Music is not transposed even when notated in high clefs." **[Consensus for critical editions]**
- **Chiavette.** Period theory asks for downward transposition of high-clef pieces. The interval is a fifth or a fourth depending on the signature (Banchieri 1601/1610; Picerli 1631), Praetorius allows a fourth for some modes, and later Italian practice moved towards the fourth (Wikipedia "Chiavette", summarising; Parrott 1984 and 2015; Rotem, Early Music Sources). How far the rule applies is still argued (Parrott against Kurtzman over Monteverdi). Rotem finds **no** period source that prescribes upward transposition of normal-clef pieces. **[Consensus on direction, Contested on interval and scope]**
- **Performance transposition.** English choral practice transposed up a minor third after Wulstan (1966–7). EECM itself now describes this as "fashionable at that time" (EECM page on vol. 3). Practitioners advise moving pitch as little as possible ("any edition more than a step throws most any singer", Hughes 2021) or pitching "where it makes the most sense for the singers you have" (forum). **[Practice; contested]**

### 1.5 Accidentals and musica ficta

- **Ficta is interpretation, not emendation.** "To add sharps and flats to a modern edition is not to 'add' what is implicit in the original notated piece. It is not emendatio but interpretatio" (Bent 2013, p. 254). Bent also writes that "Our notation requires them all the time. Theirs did not" (1994, p. 389), and that ficta "is inseparable from the contrapuntal theory and practice of its own period" (2002, p. 3). Lowinsky criticised editions with "not enough" ficta, "not applied consistently". The NJE committee, marking ficta independently for one piece, produced results of which "no two were identical" (Ory 2025, pp. 492, 501). **[Consensus that ficta must be supplied; Contested in detail]**
- **Presentation.** The Anglo-American convention puts source accidentals before the note and editorial ones above it (Lost&Found; Iberian Polyphony; Wikipedia "Musica ficta"). FMP instead puts every editorial addition in square brackets (§9). JRP publishes two versions, with and without editorial accidentals. **[Practice; either convention is acceptable if stated]**
- **Distinguish necessary from optional inflections.** Berger recommends (1) writing implied accidentals above the notes, (2) separating "indispensable" from "optional" ones, with optional ones "enclosed in parentheses", and (3) where there are two solutions, writing out both and marking the editor's preference (Berger 1987, pp. 172–88, as reported by Harrán 1990). Performers ask that "musica ficta must be identifiable as editorial" (forum). **[Strongly recommended by the leading ficta monograph; not universal]**

### 1.6 Ligatures and coloration

- Ligatures are marked with a closed horizontal bracket (A-R Style Guide p. 30; Lost&Found; FMP §9c). Coloration is marked with an **open or broken** bracket (A-R: "open horizontal brackets"; Lost&Found: "broken horizontal square bracket"). Reaney's view that ligatures merely "clutter up the transcriptions" is now a minority one. Fallows (2013) shows that ligatures also carried underlay meaning (one syllable per ligature) as well as rhythm. **[Consensus to mark ligatures; the coloration sign varies]**

### 1.7 Underlay

- Sixteenth-century rules (Lanfranco 1533; Vicentino 1555; Zarlino 1558; Stoquerus c. 1570) are the agreed starting point (Lowinsky 1962; Harrán 1973). Harrán stresses that the theorists described practice rather than legislating: Vicentino's own madrigals break his rules. **[Consensus]**
- Two specifics matter for this series:
  - **A syllable on the semiminim after a dotted minim is allowed.** Vicentino, Rule 3, after Lanfranco: "nor, moreover, after a dotted minim, if one pronounces a syllable on the black note that follows (will it be a grave mistake)" (Harrán 1973, p. 623).
  - **The ban on elision applies to Latin only.** "In Latin, elisions are never made", whereas in the vernacular "you ought to utter the second [vowel] and drop the first" (Vicentino, Rules 6 and 8; Harrán 1973, pp. 624–6).
- Sources rarely align text carefully, so underlay is usually editorial and should be signalled as such (Iberian Polyphony; Lost&Found). Bent warns that scoring replaces "horizontally-read mensural groupings" with vertical spacing and so loses the cues for underlay that partbook spacing gives (2013, pp. 249–50). **[Consensus]**

### 1.8 Text and translation

- Give "line-for-line literal translations" (not singing translations). The text in the Texts section must "be an exact match for the orthography and punctuation of the text as underlaid" (A-R pp. 8–9). FMP gives Polish **and** English translations (§2g), keeps original spelling where possible and expands abbreviations (§11). **[Consensus]**

### 1.9 Critical report

- **The method statement** must "cover all consistent alterations of the source" (A-R pp. 13–14). **Critical notes** must "report all textual and musical differences between sources and the edition that are not otherwise covered", but should not report what is already flagged in the score (A-R p. 14). Entries are given as bar, part, note number (counted from the bar line), source reading (A-R p. 15; FMP §3: "3. T; 4: d¹"). The list of corrections "ought to allow the user unambiguously to reconstruct the situation in the original source" (FMP §2d). **[Consensus]**
- **Commentary, not only apparatus.** The critical commentary is "a section often lacking in music editions"; editors should explain why, not only list variants (Grove §6). Bent wants "more discursive commentaries and music examples", and notes "on the same page as the music, where performers are more likely to take note of them" (2013, p. 256). **[Consensus in theory; practice varies]**

### 1.10 Introduction

- Grier (Grove §6): historical context; sources and their classification; "sample pages in facsimile can be useful"; editorial method; "what the edition contributes ... and its relationship to previous editions". A-R: biography, context, style, and a possible "Notes on Performance" section (p. 7); "at least two plates from the primary musical source" (p. 10). FMP: introduction, **facsimiles of selected pages**, and a source description covering location, dimensions, scribes, watermarks and dating (§2a–c). **[Consensus]**

### 1.11 What a performance edition should contain

- **Legibility.** Large enough note and font size, adequate margins, "more space between systems than there is between staves", staves labelled on the left, bar numbers "once at the start of each system", "avoid tragic page turns", and the licence "on the first page" (CPDL, *Help:The perfect edition*). **[Practice]**
- **Disclosure.** State the sources and what was changed: "If you have changed key, clefs, rhythm ... add an editorial note" (CPDL). Pair the text with a translation (CPDL; Tagg 1965 complained that translations "are not always offered"). **[Practice]**
- **Clutter.** Hughes (2021) advises against editorial slurs over melismas and against added dynamics; editorial lyrics should be in italics; ficta above the note; practice tracks where rehearsal time is short. **[Practice]**
- **Parts.** FMP makes parts available digitally (§2f). Bent argues that a well-made digital critical text can "generate customized performing parts, in various formats and transpositions" and so "remove the need for separate performing editions" (2013, p. 255). Schuiling's ethnography of vocal ensembles (2020) describes groups singing from facsimiles or partbooks as a creative practice. **[Emerging]**
- **Do not condescend to performers.** Bent: "even modern singers reading from original notation can very quickly internalise the musical grammar" (2002, p. 4). **[Scholarly view; directors of amateur choirs often disagree]**

### 1.12 Digital and open-access practice

- **Machine-readable data.** MEI is "the de facto standard for open-access electronic representations of scholarly music editions" (Stadler & Jettka 2023). It encodes variants (`app`/`lem`/`rdg` with `@source`), editorial acts (`choice`/`sic`/`corr`, `orig`/`reg`, `supplied`, `unclear`, `gap`) and responsibility (`@resp`, `@cert`) (MEI Guidelines v4, "Scholarly editing"). Projects publishing in MEI include Lost&Found (Lisbon), Lost Voices (Du Chemin), CRIM and Measuring Polyphony. JRP uses Humdrum with MusicXML along the way. **[Consensus for digital editions]**
- **Linked images.** Bent (2013, pp. 257–8) welcomes linking editions to digital images, and warns that "not all facsimiles are what they seem" (touched-up "fakesimiles"). DIAMM and Tudor Partbooks make sources available as images. **[Consensus]**
- **Open data, FAIR, persistent identifiers.** Recent work treats editions as open-data publications aligned with FAIR (Frömmel et al. 2024; Stadler & Jettka 2023). FMP puts its print-ready PDFs online in open access (Editorial standards §11). GitHub releases archived to Zenodo receive a concept DOI plus one DOI per version (GRASS GIS documentation of the GitHub–Zenodo linkage). **[Emerging consensus]**

## 2. Gap analysis against the Polish Early Music series

### 2.1 What the series already does well

- **Source-first stance and suspicion of modern editions** (principles §1). This matches Grier and Bent, and goes further than most series in collating modern editions as witnesses with sigla and treating shared errors as evidence of descent (§3.5; compare Grier's filiation by shared error).
- **Disclosure.** Every category of intervention is named in the method and signalled in the score (§§1.5, 8). The method in *Zmierzka* is compact and complete. The critical edition says what it does differently from previous editions, as Grier recommends.
- **Unreduced values, modern clefs with incipits and ambitus, source voice names.** These follow NJE, Bent 2013, CMM and FMP practice.
- **Ficta grounded in counterpoint**, with explicit grounds and a consistency rule at double leading-note cadences (§7). This is Bent's position. *Zmierzka* bar 29 shows good practice: the choice is argued, and the alternative is named.
- **Chiavette handled from period theory.** The *Vox in Rama* edition quotes Banchieri's fourth/fifth rule exactly and applies it (no signature, so a fifth down), and notes that Praetorius and Doni allowed a fourth. Rule 2.2's claim that no period source prescribes upward transposition for low clefs agrees with Rotem's survey.
- **Critical editions at written pitch**, as Bent and the Lisbon projects require.
- **Text.** The critical edition has a three-column diplomatic | transcription | translation layout. The translation is literal and line by line, as A-R asks. The edited text matches the underlay.
- **The facsimile F is described honestly** as "a redrawn copy, not a photograph". Bent's warning about facsimiles is already met.
- **Open licence (CC BY 4.0), public source repository, and a rule for numbering editions** (§14). This is ahead of most print series. The colophon makes clear how to reuse the edition.
- **Performance edition.** It has: a masthead with an explicit transposition line; translation; tempo given as a minim range; a plain explanation of the signs; no editorial dynamics, slurs or keyboard reduction; bar numbers at each system; voice labels on every system; generous staff size (19); and running heads with folios on loose sheets. All of this is CPDL and Hughes best practice.
- **Ligatures bracketed**, as in A-R, Lost&Found and FMP.

### 2.2 Where the series differs, with a defensible reason

| Practice | Ours | Others | Why ours is defensible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barring | Dashed Mensurstriche between staves only | CMM and most choral editions bar through the staff; FMP allows Mensurstriche "in justified cases" | Mensurstriche keep the source's values whole and avoid ties. JRP and Bent both support source-near presentation. The rehearsal notes explain how to read the bar lines. Some amateur choirs will find it harder (Hughes; forum). |
| Note values in the performance edition | Unreduced | Most practical editions halve (Ancient Groove; many CPDL) | NJE and Bent's position. Your rule 2.1 ("no reason to baby the performer") is a stated editorial stance, and the tempo line ("minim about 66–80") gives the means to read it. |
| Editorial accidentals | Small, above the note, no brackets | FMP uses square brackets | This is the dominant Anglo-American convention (Lost&Found, Iberian Polyphony, NJE practice). It is stated in the method. |
| Coloration | Corner brackets ⌜ ⌝ | A-R and Lost&Found use open or broken horizontal brackets | Both are recognised conventions. Yours is distinct from the ligature bracket. |
| Emendations | Silent in score, reported in notes | NMA-style silence is accepted by Grier if the policy is stated; FMP brackets every addition | Grier: "So long as editors inform their audience of their policies ... they cannot seriously be accused of misleading." |
| Performance pitch | Low-clef pieces transposed up by range analysis (*Zmierzka* a minor third) | Bent: critical editions should not transpose. Practitioners: as little as possible. | Only the performance edition transposes, it says so on the masthead, and the written-pitch score is published alongside. The minor third matches the long PWM tradition for this piece. |
| No pronunciation in each edition; one series guide, no IPA | Series guide in tables | Many choral publishers print IPA per piece | A single maintained guide avoids drift between editions. The "no IPA" rule is a judgement call (see 2.3, R14). |
| Critical notes collate modern editions | PWM, K, M readings with verdicts | A-R does not ask for readings of modern editions | For a repertory dominated by one or two inherited modern editions this is useful to performers ("why does my PWM copy differ?"), and Grier's apparatus is meant to defend choices. |

### 2.3 Prioritised recommendations

Priority: **A** = do now (cheap, or a correctness issue); **B** = next round of editions; **C** = worth considering.

**A. Correctness and consistency in the docs and editions**

- **R1. Correct underlay rule 10.1. (A)**
  - *What:* rule 10.1 forbids a syllable on "the semiminim after a dotted minim". Your own *Zmierzka* method allows exactly that ("no new syllable on a semiminim, except straight after a dotted minim"), and critical notes bars 23 and 28 apply it.
  - *Why:* the theorist the series cites allows it. Vicentino, after Lanfranco, says it is not "a grave mistake" to put a syllable on the black note after a dotted minim (Harrán 1973, p. 623). The principle and the edition contradict each other. The edition is right.
  - *Fix:* "...not on a fusa or a dot; a semiminim may take a syllable straight after a dotted minim (Lanfranco 1533; Vicentino 1555)."
  - *Source:* Harrán 1973; Lowinsky 1962. **[Consensus]**
- **R2. Limit "No elision (Vicentino 1555)" (rule 10.5) to Latin. (A)**
  - *Why:* Vicentino forbids elision in Latin but **prescribes** it in the vernacular (Rules 6 and 8; Harrán 1973, pp. 624–6). Citing him for a blanket rule misreports him.
  - *Fix:* state the Latin rule with Vicentino as authority. For Polish, give the series' own ground: Old Polish syllabic verse counts every vowel, so the line's syllable count does not allow elision, and say this is the editor's reading.
  - **[Consensus on what Vicentino says]**
- **R3. Test later stanzas for stress before declaring the underlay identical. (A)**
  - *What:* the *Zmierzka* method says later stanzas "take the notes of stanza 1 syllable for syllable" because the lines are octosyllabic. Equal syllable count does not guarantee that stresses fall in the same places, and rule 10.14 says a stanza with a melisma "on a weak syllable" must be underlaid.
  - *Example (my reading, to be checked):* in line 1 the Cantus melisma falls on syllable 6 (*nad-CHO-dzi*). In stanza 2 syllable 6 is the unstressed *-nie* of *PA-nie*. Run a stress table for each stanza and voice against the melismas.
  - *Then:* underlay the stanzas that differ, or add one rehearsal sentence on where to move syllables.
  - *Why:* this is your own rule 10.14, and A-R requires the printed text to "match ... the text as underlaid". Practitioners ask for "consistent syllabification" (forum). **[Consensus that underlay must fit stress; stanza layout is Practice]**
- **R4. Make the verdict formula consistent. (A, trivial)**
  - Rule 3.8 offers SOURCE RESTORED or [EDITOR] ACCEPTED. *Zmierzka* bar 29 has "A kept".
  - Either add SOURCE KEPT, for a reading kept against modern editions when no editor emended the source, or use SOURCE RESTORED.

**B. Scholarly apparatus**

- **R5. Add facsimile plates to every critical edition where the images are public domain. (A/B)**
  - *What:* at least one page, ideally the opening of each voice. For *Zmierzka*, F (Polona) is public domain under your own §14.5. Caption it with source, partbook or voice, folio and shelfmark.
  - *Why:* A-R requires "at least two plates"; FMP requires "facsimiles of selected manuscript pages"; Grier: "sample pages in facsimile can be useful"; Bent wants editions linked to images. The *Zmierzka* underlay argument rests on F's spacing ("gaps mark melismas"), which a reader cannot check without the image.
  - **[Consensus]**
- **R6. Collate every surviving witness, textual ones included, before calling an edition final. (B)**
  - *What:* *Zmierzka* lists S (Seklucjan 1559, the tune) as "not yet collated". The introduction also names Zaremba's *Pieśni chwał boskich* (1558) as a witness to the **text**, but it is not among the sources.
  - *Why:* A-R: "identify all known sources, describe relationships among them". FMP §7: discuss differences between all records and the principal source. Grier: the edition's choice of base text needs the stemma. The tune and text witnesses bear directly on the Tenor and on the stanzas (R3).
  - *Note:* rule 2.5 names gaps in one sentence, but a primary witness that can be reached is better collated than named.
  - **[Consensus]**
- **R7. Add note-position numbers to the critical notes, and state that the notes let the reader reconstruct the source. (B)**
  - *What:* use bar | voice | note number in the bar, as A-R does ("notes ... counted consecutively from the beginning of the measure ... rests ... separately") and FMP does ("3. T; 4: d¹"). Breve bars with semiminim runs make "bar 23 A" ambiguous.
  - *Add to the method:* "Together with the signs in the score, the critical notes record every departure from the base text", which is FMP's "unambiguously reconstruct" standard. This matters because emendations are silent in the score.
  - **[Consensus]**
- **R8. Mark optional ficta differently from necessary ficta, and give alternatives in the score. (B)**
  - *What:* adopt Berger's recommendation: necessary editorial accidentals as now; **optional** ones (where period rules conflict, or a modern edition's inflection is defensible but not preferred) in round brackets above the note. *Zmierzka* bar 29 is the model case: the critical note says "Singers who prefer the flat may take it", but the score does not show that the choice exists.
  - *Rules to update:* §7 and §8. The house-style table already uses round brackets for "editorial suggestion (performance edition only)". Either share that sign, which risks confusion, or choose a separate one, for example a bracketed small accidental in **both** editions.
  - *Why:* Berger 1987, pp. 172–88; the NJE committee experience ("no two were identical", Ory 2025, p. 501) shows how far reasonable editors differ; performers ask that ficta be "identifiable as editorial".
  - **[Strongly recommended; Contested in detail]**
- **R9. Put short notes for performers on the music page where it changes what they sing. (B)**
  - *What:* in the performance edition, a footnote or margin note at bar 29: "b or b♭; see For rehearsal".
  - *Why:* Bent 2013, p. 256: notes "on the same page as the music, where performers are more likely to take note of them". Grier: editors may "present information of immediate concern to the performer on the page".
  - *Design:* a one-line foot-of-page note in small italic breaks no house-style rule (no boxes, no colour).
  - **[Consensus in principle]**
- **R10. Expand the source description to the codicological minimum. (B)**
  - *What:* for every source seen, add format and dimensions, collation or foliation, RISM siglum and number (A/I or B/I), VD16 number for prints, and the copy and shelfmark used.
  - *Why:* FMP §2c and its citation norms (RISM and VD16 numbers, copy and shelfmark); A-R p. 11. *Zmierzka*'s A entry is good on gatherings and clefs but has no RISM or VD16 references or dimensions. The lost print may still carry a bibliographical number.
  - **[Consensus]**

**C. Performance edition and access**

- **R11. Publish the performance edition at written pitch as well as transposed. (B)**
  - *What:* when the performance edition is transposed, also generate a performance-format PDF at written pitch. Today the masthead tells a choir that wants written pitch to "use the critical edition", whose score is buried at pp. 7–8 and set at staff size 17.
  - *Why:* Bent 2013, p. 255 (digital texts should generate "various formats and transpositions"). Hughes: singers with absolute pitch are "thrown" by transposition. Directors differ on pitch (forum). Your pipeline already transposes automatically (`pol-ren.ily`), so the cost is one build target.
  - **[Practice; strongly supported]**
- **R12. Offer single-voice parts (PDF), and optionally MIDI rehearsal files. (B/C)**
  - *Why:* FMP §2f supplies parts digitally. Bent 2013 favours generated parts. Schuiling 2020 documents ensembles that sing from parts or facsimiles. A partbook layout also suits your house style ("the sixteenth-century partbook, read with modern eyes"). Hughes recommends practice tracks for choirs short of rehearsal time, and LilyPond can write MIDI from the same source.
  - **[Practice]**
- **R13. Print the licence and the edition identifier on page 1 of the performance edition. (A, trivial)**
  - *What:* choirs photocopy the music pages. Today the CC BY line and the source of the copy are only on the last page. Add a one-line footer to page 1: "Polish Early Music 3 · Performance edition, 1st edn 2026 · CC BY 4.0 · [URL/DOI]".
  - *Why:* CPDL: "Print the licensing terms ... on the first page."
  - **[Practice]**
- **R14. Reconsider the "no IPA" rule in the pronunciation guide. (C)**
  - *What:* keep the comparison-with-familiar-words tables, and add an IPA column for international choirs who already work from IPA-based diction books.
  - *Caveat:* I found no published standard that requires IPA, and I could not check whether McGee et al., *Singing Early Music* (1996) covers Polish. Treat this as a judgement call, not a gap against best practice.
  - **[Practice; unsourced]**

**D. Digital and open-access practice**

- **R15. Publish machine-readable music alongside the PDFs. (B)**
  - *What:* MEI is the scholarly standard (MEI Guidelines; Lost&Found publishes MEI next to its PDFs). MusicXML is what performers can import into notation software (CPDL editors, Hughes).
  - *How:* LilyPond 2.24 has no native MEI or MusicXML export, so a converter or a dual-encoding workflow would be needed. Options include python-ly's experimental MusicXML export, or making the data in MEI or Humdrum and generating LilyPond from it. Encode your editorial categories in MEI: `supplied` for editorial notes, `choice/sic/corr` for silent emendations, `app/rdg` with `@source` for the PWM/K/M readings, and `@resp` for editor and translator. Your sign table (§8) maps onto these elements almost one to one.
  - *Note:* the toolchain advice is mine; the MEI recommendation is the field's.
  - **[Consensus for digital editions]**
- **R16. Mint DOIs and version each release. (B)**
  - *What:* connect the repository to Zenodo so that each tagged release ("Zmierzka 1st edn 2026") gets a version DOI under one concept DOI. Print the DOI and the release tag in both colophons, with a "How to cite" line. Keep a short corrections list per edition so the rule "corrections that change readings make a new edition" can be checked.
  - *Why:* FAIR and open-data practice for editions (Frömmel et al. 2024; Stadler & Jettka 2023); the GitHub–Zenodo concept/version DOI model.
  - **[Emerging consensus]**
- **R17. Keep build artefacts out of edition folders. (A, housekeeping)**
  - *What:* `editions/juz-sie-zmierzka/` holds over 20 stray `tmp*.pdf`/`tmp*.out` files, and `nunc-scio-vere/` holds more. Remove them or ignore them in git, so that the public repository shows only content and released PDFs. It is the published record of the edition.
  - This is not a scholarly-practice point; I noticed it while looking at the files.

### 2.4 Contested matters where I recommend no change, only a sentence of justification

- **Mensurstriche in the performance edition.** Some practitioners object. Your rehearsal note already explains them. If amateur choirs report trouble, the cheapest fix is R11 or R12 (parts), not changing the score.
- **Unreduced values for performers.** This is defensible (NJE, Bent). Keep the tempo-in-minims line; it is the key aid.
- **Corner brackets for coloration.** These are fine, but say in the method that they differ from A-R/Lisbon practice, to help readers who know those series.
- **Recordings as sigla for accidentals and underlay "as heard"** (§3.6). This is unusual. Keep it clearly subordinate, as you do; no series I found does it. The critical notes should never cite a recording as the *ground* for a reading.
